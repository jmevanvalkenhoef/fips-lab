One model for every cut

The data are – of the cuts of a–b from write-up 01. For – of them I know how long after start-up the cut came; the other – were in a mesh that had been running for an unknown time. The same model covers every cut, and its parameters are fitted to the data:

Rerouted
a reaches b again after the dead-link timeout plus a delay. The delay is usually short (fast) and sometimes tens of seconds (slow). The share of slow reroutes is estimated separately for cuts inside the window and for a warm mesh.
Stuck
Possible only if the cut comes before the window closes, W seconds after start-up. Inside the window, the chance of sticking may rise or fall with the time of the cut (a logistic curve).
Back after being stuck
A stuck cut recovers when the window closes, at W after start-up give or take a few seconds, whenever the cut was made.

The prior on W is flat from 0 to 20 minutes: nothing in the model knows about 300 s. A cut that hit the 45 s wait without rerouting is kept as a censored observation: the model learns only that it took at least that long, and weighs both ways that can happen (stuck, or very slow).

Predicted outages

The posterior can also be used for simulation. For a cut at any moment after start-up, simulating the model's draws gives the whole distribution of how long a is cut off from b, with the uncertainty about every parameter folded in.

Inside the window, the time since start-up matters more for the outage than the timeout does. A cut that sticks waits for the window to close, so the cost of sticking shrinks as the window runs down: a cut at 290 s is all but certain to stick, and loses about ten seconds when it does. Limits of the model

The sampler diagnostics are good: no fit has an R̂ above –, the smallest effective sample size is –, and – of 12 000 draws diverged. Getting there took two fixes. The spread of the return around W needed a floor of one second (the reachability wait's probe interval): without it, fits that see no return push the spread to zero, and the sampler diverges at the sharp edges that censoring creates. And the delay mixture has a second, worse mode, one wide component with the other squeezed against it, about 10 log units below the main one; one chain in four started at random stayed there for the whole run. The chains now start from the data's own median delay, and all four agree.

The observed counts sit inside the model's 90% intervals (the table under the second chart), the 91 s group at the top edge. So the model fits the data. Its limits come from what it leaves out:

  • One mesh and one link. Six nodes in a ring, always the a–b link. W is a property of FIPS and should carry over; the chance of sticking depends on which node ends up holding a stale entry, and will not.
  • The shape of the risk curve is assumed. Logistic in time, measured at five points inside the window, most of them with few cuts.
  • The model has no explanation for the slow reroutes. They cluster at 16 s, and the model's single in-window slow share smears them over the whole window.
  • Warm cuts have no clock. Their time after start-up is unknown, so they teach the model about delays and nothing about the window.

With these limits, the model found a timer it was never told about from – timed cuts, within a few seconds of the value in the source, and it reports how uncertain each part of the answer is. The posterior also showed where new data would help most, so the test cuts went where the band was widest.
